# ADR-0014: the order-leg courier compensation's clearing claim — the dedicated delivery journal's cross-journal funding model

- **Status:** Accepted (closes the 2026-11-14 adversarial behavioral review's finding **F-832-2** — the P2 model-consistency question raised against this branch's A1/D4 execution; the review's documented remedy option «a documented ADR» is the option adopted here)
- **Governing references:**
  - [ADR-0013](ADR-0013-financial-allocation-decisions.md) — D1 (the platform is the merchant of record: ONE payment engine owns every intent — one charge, one collection), D3 (payout is a separate operational stage; ledger balances are bookkeeping, not stored value), and D4 (the launch mode is the platform-OPERATED delivery fleet with an OSRM-compatible routing provider — the mode the fee funds; the fee-retention funding policy itself is THIS record's own decision, grounded in D1's merchant-of-record posture: the platform's own charge funds the platform's own fleet — no ADR-0013 decision states the retention, and an attribution must name a decision that actually says what it is cited for)
  - The merged architecture — [platform-wide payments and delivery](../architecture/platform-wide-payments-and-delivery.md) (the per-origin journal boundaries and the delivery vertical's dedicated journal)
  - The measured code on this branch — `LedgerJournalService` (the DIRECT-leg posting chain and `compensateCarrier`'s own javadoc contract) and `LedgerPaymentEventListener` (the ORDER settlement's D4 split and the fee-reversal mirror)
  - The 2026-11-14 adversarial review (F-832-2) — the precise concern this record answers

## Context

The A1 execution of ADR-0013's D4 split works across two ledgers. At an ORDER's settlement, the provider-balance ledger (the seller's statement) credits the payment intent's FULL total and then debits the commission **and the delivery fee** — the fee deduction is what leaves the goods value in the balance (for a 17400 total with a 4000 fee, the pre-commission balance is 13400, never a double-deducted 9400) — the platform retains the fee to fund the carrier (D4 verbatim). The carrier's own earn is booked separately, in the **dedicated delivery journal**, by `LedgerJournalService.compensateCarrier`: one pair, `Dr PSP_CLEARING / Cr DELIVERY_COURIER_PAYABLE`, keyed `CARRIER_COMPENSATION` on the delivery request id, bound to the **official payment intent id** — the ORDER intent that actually collected the money (the event binding this branch added).

The adversarial review measured the model-consistency gap (F-832-2): the ORDER path creates no local `CAPTURE` entry in the delivery journal at all — `capture()` serves only DELIVERY-origin intents by its own contract, and it writes `Dr PSP_CLEARING / Cr DELIVERY_CUSTOMER_ADVANCES` (clearing on the DEBIT side; `refund()` is the entry that carries `PSP_CLEARING` on the credit side) — so the ORDER-leg compensation debits clearing with **no journal-local credit leg** from the ORDER path. Read journal-internally, `PSP_CLEARING` accumulates order-leg debits that cannot settle inside the journal. The review offered two remedies: reclassify the debit leg as an explicit platform hold, or document the model in an ADR.

## Decision

The posting stays `Dr PSP_CLEARING / Cr DELIVERY_COURIER_PAYABLE`. In the dedicated delivery journal, `PSP_CLEARING` is a **claim account against the physical PSP collection pot** — not a journal-local escrow that must balance within one journal. The same physical pot has two documented funding paths into the carrier's payable:

1. **DIRECT deliveries** book the collection inside this journal (`CAPTURE: Dr CLEARING / Cr ADVANCES`, then `RECOGNIZE`, then the `REVENUE → COURIER_PAYABLE` leg) — the delivery intent is itself the collecting intent, so the capture exists locally.
2. **ORDER-leg deliveries** ride the ORDER intent's single charge (D1: one payment engine, one collection). Its seller-side allocation is booked in the provider-balance ledger — first the gross `PAYMENT_CREDIT` for the full ORDER intent amount, then the commission debit and the `DELIVERY_FEE` debit. For a 17,400 intent with a 4,000 fee, the fee is deducted once, leaving a 13,400 pre-commission goods basis — not a goods-only credit followed by a second fee deduction. The delivery journal's compensation pair is the **application** of that funding, and its debit names `PSP_CLEARING` because the courier is paid from collected money, not from an escrow the platform opened for the purpose.

**Cross-journal tie:** the `officialPaymentIntentId` — the compensation pair's payment reference is the ORDER intent that collected the funds. The fee's two records name the same intent from both sides: the seller's `DELIVERY_FEE` debit (the platform's retained hold on the seller's proceeds — the disclosable platform-hold concept lives there) and the journal's clearing-claim debit (the earmarking of collected money for the courier). The reversal discipline is symmetric on both sides: a full refund reverses the fee debit on the seller's statement (`creditFromDeliveryFeeReversal`, reading the posted row's own amount — the F-832-1 mirror) and the fee-reversal reconciliation sweep converges pre-mirror stragglers.

**Grounding:** THIS record's own funding decision — «the platform keeps the fee to fund the carrier» is the policy this ADR itself adopts (its title's own subject: the cross-journal funding model), grounded in D1's merchant-of-record posture (the fee rides the platform's own single charge, so retaining it is the platform allocating its own collection, not the seller's) and D4's mode selection (the platform-OPERATED fleet is what the fee funds); D3's — payout is a separate operational stage and balances are bookkeeping, not stored value (the journal earmarks; the physical settlement is the payout stage); D1's — one collection event per order, so there is no second capture to book. The code's measured contracts agree: `capture()`'s origin guard is the DIRECT-leg contract, and `compensateCarrier`'s javadoc already records the order-leg binding (the S12 lesson: a legal event with no legal consumer — the pre-fix shape that routed every completion into `recognize` and failed forever on the order-leg's by-design null intent).

## Consequences

- The journal's `PSP_CLEARING` account runs a growing debit for ORDER-leg compensations **by design**; it settles operationally against the physical PSP account at the payout stage (D3), never journal-internally. Reconciliation between the two records is by payment intent id — the same identifier both legs carry.
- The alternative (a `PLATFORM_HOLD` debit account) was considered and rejected for this execution: it would split the physical pot's claim semantics across two names without moving a single unit of money, while the platform-hold concept already has its disclosable home — the seller's `DELIVERY_FEE` debit on the provider-balance statement.
- No money-movement change, no migration, no new account: this record is the documentation remedy the review sanctioned; the posting, its idempotency key discipline (`CARRIER_COMPENSATION` per delivery request), and its atomic binding to the credit write's own outcome (round 3) are unchanged.
- The later external-carrier mode (D4's Phase 4) that introduces per-carrier settlement accounts will carry its own ADR; nothing here precludes it — this record covers the platform-operated launch mode's dedicated journal only.
